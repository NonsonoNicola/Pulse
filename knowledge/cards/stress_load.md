## Early signs
Irritability, low mood, reduced energy, poorer concentration, sleep problems, social withdrawal, and a drop in heart-rate variability. These signs overlap with illness and poor sleep.

## Typical evolution
Short-term stress can be adaptive. When it persists, sleep and mood deteriorate and performance becomes less reliable. Crew conflict or isolation can amplify the cycle.

## Environmental factors
Workload, schedule pressure, isolation, confinement, communication delay, family separation, and major mission events. Check whether several crew members are affected by the same operational pressure.

## What to check (tests and measures)
Mood and energy check-ins, sleep, workload and schedule, recent mission events, social climate. Use a neutral question rather than an accusation.

## First actions
Reduce non-essential workload, protect sleep, schedule a short break, encourage peer support, and involve the CMO or behavioural support early.

## Escalate when
There is hopelessness, self-harm language, inability to perform safety-critical duties, severe anxiety, or persistent deterioration over more than a week. Follow the mental-health crisis protocol and open a priority channel to ground.

## Do not
Do not dismiss symptoms as “just stress”. Do not promise confidentiality the system cannot guarantee. Do not use gamification or competition during a crisis.

## Plain-language summary (for non-medical crew)
Stress is a health signal, not a score. If your mood, sleep or focus is slipping, tell the CMO early and reduce the load.

## Required resources (inventory)
Private communication channel, CMO support, scheduled rest.
