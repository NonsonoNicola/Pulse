## Early signs
Pain, swelling, bruising, limited movement, an open wound, bleeding, or a change in sensation. Minor injuries can become serious if contaminated or if circulation is compromised.

## Typical evolution
Most minor injuries heal with cleaning, dressing and rest. Infection, compartment syndrome, fracture, or uncontrolled bleeding require urgent escalation. Microgravity can mask swelling.

## Environmental factors
EVA suit injuries, exercise equipment, tools, falls during transfers, reduced bone density, and limited imaging access.

## What to check (tests and measures)
Wound size and depth, bleeding, contamination, distal circulation and sensation, range of motion, pain severity, and tetanus status. Use ultrasound only if trained and the result will change management.

## First actions
Control bleeding with pressure, clean and dress the wound, immobilise a suspected fracture, elevate if possible, and contact the CMO. Record the time and mechanism.

## Escalate when
Bleeding does not stop, the wound is deep or contaminated, there is numbness or loss of circulation, severe pain, deformity, or signs of infection (redness, warmth, pus, fever).

## Do not
Do not close a heavily contaminated wound without CMO review. Do not give antibiotics without CMO direction. Do not provide dose numbers in free text.

## Plain-language summary (for non-medical crew)
Stop the bleeding, clean and cover the wound, and protect the injured area. Tell the CMO about numbness, severe pain or bleeding that will not stop.

## Required resources (inventory)
Sterile dressings, bandages, splint, wound cleaning supplies, ultrasound if trained, medication cabinet.
