## Early signs
Persistent low mood, loss of interest, sleep change, irritability, withdrawal, hopelessness, anxiety, or expressions of being a burden. Direct or indirect statements about self-harm require immediate attention.

## Typical evolution
Distress can build silently under mission pressure. Early supportive contact reduces isolation. A crisis may present as physical symptoms, conflict, or refusal to report.

## Environmental factors
Isolation, confinement, communication delay, family stress, workload, sleep loss, and crew conflict. Asking directly about safety does not increase risk.

## What to check (tests and measures)
Ask calmly about mood, sleep, support, and safety. Use a private channel. Screen for self-harm thoughts and immediate risk. Document only what is needed for care.

## First actions
Stay with the person if there is immediate risk, listen without judgment, involve the CMO, activate the mental-health crisis protocol, and open a priority channel to ground. Offer practical support and follow-up.

## Escalate when
There is any current intent or plan for self-harm, severe agitation, psychosis, inability to care for self, or risk to the crew. Treat as red and do not leave the person alone.

## Do not
Do not promise absolute confidentiality. Do not minimise or debate the feelings. Do not use gamification, scoring or performance language. Do not share details beyond those who need them for care.

## Plain-language summary (for non-medical crew)
If you or a crewmate feel hopeless or think about self-harm, tell the CMO now. Support and a private link to ground are available. This is a health issue, not a failure.

## Required resources (inventory)
Private communication channel, CMO support, behavioural health guidance, scheduled follow-up.
