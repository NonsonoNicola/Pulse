## Early signs
Headache, fatigue, reduced sleep quality, difficulty concentrating, lower heart-rate variability, irritability. Symptoms are non-specific and often appear in several crew members at once.

## Typical evolution
With sustained exposure, headaches become more frequent and sleep efficiency falls. Cognitive performance and mood may decline gradually. A shared pattern across the crew is more suggestive than an isolated symptom.

## Environmental factors
Cabin CO2 concentration, scrubber performance, ventilation flow, module occupancy, exercise load, and duration of exposure. Compare crew trends with the environmental log rather than a single reading.

## What to check (tests and measures)
Daily CO2 trend and scrubber status; symptom check-ins from all crew; sleep duration and efficiency; resting heart rate and HRV. Consider a second CO2 sensor if readings conflict.

## First actions
Increase ventilation, reduce strenuous activity, check the CO2 scrubber and airflow, move the crew to a better-ventilated module if needed. Report the pattern to the CMO.

## Escalate when
Persistent headache with confusion, vomiting, severe drowsiness, or a crew-wide deterioration. Escalate if CO2 remains elevated after corrective actions.

## Do not
Do not treat this as a personal weakness or assume a single astronaut is affected. Do not ignore a crew-wide pattern.

## Plain-language summary (for non-medical crew)
Bad air can make the whole crew feel tired and headachy. If several people feel worse at once, check the air system before treating each person separately.

## Required resources (inventory)
Cabin CO2 sensor, scrubber controls, ventilation controls.
