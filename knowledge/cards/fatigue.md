## Early signs
Unusual fatigue, poor sleep, reduced motivation, heavier perceived exertion, higher resting heart rate, lower heart-rate variability, and slower recovery after exercise.

## Typical evolution
Fatigue can be driven by sleep debt, workload, illness or inadequate recovery between exercise sessions. Persistent fatigue with fever, breathlessness, chest pain, or mood deterioration needs medical review.

## Environmental factors
Exercise countermeasure load, schedule density, sleep disruption, CO2, dehydration, and psychological stress. Microgravity changes the usual muscle-load signals.

## What to check (tests and measures)
Sleep and workload log, hydration, resting heart rate and HRV trend, temperature, oxygen saturation, and recent exercise load. Review the last two weeks, not a single day.

## First actions
Reduce non-essential load, protect sleep, hydrate, and adjust exercise intensity. Schedule a reassessment rather than pushing through.

## Escalate when
Fatigue worsens despite rest, is accompanied by fever, chest pain, breathlessness, fainting, or low mood, or interferes with safety-critical tasks.

## Do not
Do not treat persistent fatigue as a motivation problem. Do not increase training to “catch up”.

## Plain-language summary (for non-medical crew)
Being unusually tired for several days is data, not weakness. Cut the load, sleep, drink, and ask the CMO to review if it does not improve.

## Required resources (inventory)
Wearable, thermometer, blood pressure cuff, scale.
