## Early signs
Chest pressure, squeezing, burning, or heaviness; pain radiating to the arm, jaw or back; breathlessness; sweating; nausea; fainting; palpitations. Symptoms may be atypical in younger or female crew members.

## Typical evolution
Cardiac events can deteriorate quickly. Other causes include reflux, muscle strain, anxiety and pulmonary embolism. The initial response must assume a serious cause until assessed.

## Environmental factors
Microgravity cardiovascular changes, radiation, dehydration, heat, exertion, and delayed access to definitive care.

## What to check (tests and measures)
Vital signs, oxygen saturation, ECG if available, blood pressure in both arms if feasible, breathing effort, skin colour, and response to rest. Record the exact timeline.

## First actions
Stop activity, call the CMO, sit or recline, measure vitals and ECG, prepare oxygen and the defibrillator, and start the chest-pain protocol. Generate an SBAR packet immediately.

## Escalate when
Ongoing chest pain, breathlessness, low oxygen, fainting, abnormal ECG, severe sweating, or instability. Treat as red and involve ground as soon as the link allows.

## Do not
Do not wait for the LLM or for ground. Do not give aspirin unless the CMO confirms it is appropriate and there is no allergy or bleeding risk. Do not provide medication doses in free text.

## Plain-language summary (for non-medical crew)
Chest pressure is an emergency until proven otherwise. Stop, call the CMO, measure your oxygen and ECG, and follow the protocol step by step. The ground team can wait; you cannot.

## Required resources (inventory)
ECG, oxygen supply, defibrillator, blood pressure cuff, stethoscope, aspirin (only with CMO confirmation), medication cabinet.
