## Early signs
Shortness of breath, rapid breathing, low oxygen saturation, chest tightness, cough, blue lips, confusion, or inability to speak in full sentences. A sensor that disagrees with other signals must be checked, not trusted blindly.

## Typical evolution
Respiratory compromise can worsen quickly. Possible causes include infection, asthma-like reaction, pulmonary embolism, CO2 exposure, oxygen system failure, and sensor error. Reassess after each intervention.

## Environmental factors
Cabin oxygen and CO2, scrubber failure, smoke or contaminants, pressure changes, exertion, and altitude or gravity transitions.

## What to check (tests and measures)
Repeat SpO2 with a second device, respiratory rate, heart rate, work of breathing, breath sounds, temperature, CO2 reading, and response to rest. Record the sensor used.

## First actions
Sit upright, stop activity, repeat the measurement, check the oxygen supply and cabin environment, call the CMO, and follow the low-oxygen protocol.

## Escalate when
Oxygen remains low, breathing is laboured, confusion or fainting occurs, or the person cannot speak normally. Escalate immediately and prepare an SBAR packet.

## Do not
Do not dismiss low oxygen as a sensor error without a second measurement. Do not delay CMO contact. Do not give oxygen flow rates or medication doses outside the protocol.

## Plain-language summary (for non-medical crew)
If you cannot breathe normally, sit up, repeat the oxygen reading with another device, and call the CMO now. Do not wait to see if it passes.

## Required resources (inventory)
Pulse oximeter, oxygen supply, stethoscope, ECG, blood pressure cuff.
