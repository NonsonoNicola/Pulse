## Early signs
Head pressure or pain, light or noise sensitivity, nausea, and reduced concentration. A pattern across the crew points to a shared environmental cause.

## Typical evolution
Most headaches are benign and improve with rest, hydration and correcting the environment. A sudden severe headache, headache with neurological signs, fever, chest pain, or breathlessness needs urgent assessment.

## Environmental factors
CO2, poor ventilation, dehydration, sleep loss, noise, stress, and altered light cycles. Migraine history increases susceptibility.

## What to check (tests and measures)
Onset, location, severity, associated nausea or visual changes, hydration, sleep, CO2 trend, blood pressure and temperature when indicated. Ask about new medications.

## First actions
Rest in a quiet, well-ventilated area, hydrate, reduce screen exposure, and correct obvious environmental triggers. Use only CMO-approved analgesia.

## Escalate when
Sudden severe headache, headache after a head injury, fever with neck stiffness, neurological deficit, confusion, chest pain, or persistent vomiting.

## Do not
Do not repeatedly treat a recurring headache without looking for a shared environmental cause. Do not give medication doses in free text.

## Plain-language summary (for non-medical crew)
Most headaches improve with rest, water and better air. A sudden worst-ever headache or one with confusion, fever or weakness needs immediate CMO help.

## Required resources (inventory)
CO2 sensor, thermometer, blood pressure cuff, medication cabinet.
